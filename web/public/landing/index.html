Forty wolves, and nobody gets hurt.

Chapter 12 of The Wonderful Wizard of Oz is a lot for a five-year-old at bedtime. Here is the original beside a read-aloud edition for age 6, with violence set to Soften. Changed words are highlighted, just like in Featherbound's sample review.

Original Violence · intense

“This is my fight,” said the Woodman, “so get behind me and I will meet them as they come.”

He seized his axe, which he had made very sharp, and as the leader of the wolves came on the Tin Woodman swung his arm and chopped the wolf’s head from its body, so that it immediately died. As soon as he could raise his axe another wolf came up, and he also fell under the sharp edge of the Tin Woodman’s weapon. There were forty wolves, and forty times a wolf was killed, so that at last they all lay dead in a heap before the Woodman.

“This is my fight,” said the Woodman, “so get behind me and I will meet them as they come.”

The wolf tumbled head over tail and ran yelping back the way it had come. As soon as he could raise his axe another wolf came up, and CLANG! went the axe again. There were forty wolves, and forty times the axe rang out, so that at last every one of them had scampered off over the hill.

How it works

From your bookshelf to their bedtime in five steps.

  1. 1

    Import a book you own

    Drop in a DRM-free EPUB or PDF. Featherbound finds the chapters, cover, series and genre, and you check them before it goes on the shelf.

  2. 2

    See what's inside

    A chapter-by-chapter content report across nine categories, plus a Story Bible of the characters, places and plot threads.

  3. 3

    Choose your settings

    A short wizard: the reader's age, read-aloud or independent, and keep, soften or remove for each kind of content. Add notes in your own words.

  4. 4

    Review two sample chapters

    The first chapter and the most intense one, side by side with the original. Highlight anything and comment until it reads right.

  5. 5

    Get the whole book

    Featherbound writes every chapter, paragraph for paragraph, then checks the whole book for consistency. Read it in the app or export it.

What's inside

Know a book before your child does.

Before you change anything, Featherbound reads the book through and tells you what's in it, chapter by chapter. Then you decide, category by category, what to keep, what to soften and what to take out.

The categories follow the ones Common Sense Media uses. Each starts from a sensible default for your reader's age, and you can change any of them.

Picture Book mode

Pictures drawn in the book's own style.

Turn on pictures and Featherbound studies how the book already looks: its original illustrations, its cover, or how an earlier book in the series was drawn. Then it draws new pictures to match, as often as you like.

  • Moments chosen as the text is written

    Pick a density, from one picture a chapter to one every few pages, and see the cost before you start.

  • Too-scary originals, redrawn

    If one of the book's own pictures is too intense for the edition, it's redrawn to match the softened text.

  • Yours to change

    Redraw a picture, upload your own, or remove it. Each change is saved as a new version.

For parents

Made for the grown-up holding the book.

🔒

Hand-off mode

Lock a phone or tablet to one edition and hand it over. Unlocking means pressing and holding Tuft, then entering your PIN. The lock is enforced on the server and survives the app being closed.

🪶

Refine any time

Ask for a change to the whole book, a few chapters or one passage. Every change is a new version you can compare and roll back.

📖

A reader they'll open

Page turns, two-page spreads, themes and dyslexia-friendly fonts, with progress synced across devices. Or export an EPUB for e-readers, or a 6 × 9 inch PDF to print.

🧾

No cost surprises

An estimate before every run, a cost cap that pauses at the end of a chapter, and a monthly budget dashboard.

📚

Grows with your kids

Make an edition for age 6 now and another for age 9 later. Each one shows the settings it was made with.

Costs

Pennies a chapter, on your own key.

Featherbound doesn't charge anything. You add your own AI key in Settings and pay the providers' list prices directly. Keys are encrypted and never sent to the browser.

Figures are from real runs on a 39,000-word book with Claude Opus 5.5. Every run shows its estimate first and stops at the cap you set.

Questions

Good questions, honest answers.

Does it shorten the book or change what happens?

No. Editions are always full length, paragraph for paragraph. Events can be softened or moved off the page, but what happens in the story never changes, and a consistency check across the whole book rewrites any chapter that drifts.

Which books can I use?

Books you own, as DRM-free EPUB or PDF files. Featherbound doesn't remove DRM, and if a file is locked it tells you so kindly. You're responsible for having the right to any book you import.

Can I share the editions I make?

No. Editions are private copies for your own family. There's no public sharing, and nothing is shared between accounts: each library, key and setting is its own.

Do my kids need accounts?

No. Kids never sign in, and Featherbound keeps no record of them. You read to them, or hand them a device locked to one edition with Hand-off mode.

Should I still read it first?

Yes. Adaptations are written by AI. The checks keep the plot and characters intact, but a parent should still read what their child will read. That's exactly what sample review is for.

How do I get access?

Featherbound is in a closed beta: only invited accounts can sign in, and we aren't taking invite requests right now. It's also MIT-licensed, so you can run your own copy on Vercel today. The README walks you through setup.
